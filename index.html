Slime Polymer Chemistry

Scientific mechanism behind PVA polymer chain cross-linking by Borate ions.

Polymer Cross-Linking Mechanism:

PVA (Polyvinyl Alcohol) consists of long linear chains containing hydroxyl (-OH) functional groups. In liquid state, these polymer chains slide past one another easily. When Borax is added to water, it dissociates into Borate ions [B(OH)₄]⁻. These borate ions form hydrogen and ionic bonds connecting adjacent PVA chains together. This 3D cross-linked network traps water molecules and restricts free molecular sliding, transforming the liquid solution into an elastic, viscoelastic gel (slime).

Chemical Principle:

Slime forms mainly because borax creates cross-links between polymer chains present in the PVA-based glue. These cross-links reduce the free movement of polymer molecules, producing a flexible and elastic gel-like material.

About Project

Interactive 3D Chemical Laboratory Simulation

This interactive 3D laboratory project was designed to visually demonstrate the step-by-step chemical preparation of polymer slime in a virtual laboratory environment.

Built using HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, Three.js, and OrbitControls, it features real-time WebGL rendering, lathed borosilicate glass beaker geometry, deformable slime mesh with trapped micro-bubbles, CAD inspection view modes, and procedural sound synthesis.
